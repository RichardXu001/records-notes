Notes on the paperwork that travels with laboratory material: the receiving sheet written at the door, the lot number printed on the label, the storage log kept on a clipboard by the fridge, and the note drafted on the day something does not match.

What these notes are

Each note describes a document a laboratory keeps anyway, and asks a narrow question about it: which fields get written honestly, which get filled in by habit, and which turn out to be the only thing anyone reads when a question arrives months later. The answers come from the shape of the page rather than from a particular product, and nothing here is specific to one supplier.

Readers who keep receiving sheets, transfer lists, or temperature logs will recognise the tension. A form with too few fields loses the detail that made it worth keeping; a form with too many gets abandoned halfway through a delivery. The four notes below stay on the side of a page that someone will still finish at the end of a working day.
